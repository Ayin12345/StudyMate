Physics — Classical Mechanics Notes

Kinematics
Displacement is the change in position (vector); distance is the total path length (scalar).
Velocity = displacement / time. Speed = distance / time.
Acceleration = change in velocity / time.
Kinematic equations (constant acceleration):
  v = v₀ + at
  x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²
  v² = v₀² + 2a(x - x₀)
Free fall: a = -g ≈ -9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface.

Newton's Laws of Motion
First law (inertia): an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at constant velocity, unless acted on by a net external force.
Second law: F = ma. Net force equals mass times acceleration.
Third law: for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
Weight = mg (force of gravity on a mass).
Normal force: the support force perpendicular to a surface.
Friction: f = μN, where μ is the coefficient of friction (static or kinetic).

Work, Energy, and Power
Work = F · d · cos(θ), where θ is the angle between force and displacement.
Kinetic energy: KE = ½mv².
Gravitational potential energy: PE = mgh.
Conservation of energy: in the absence of friction, total mechanical energy (KE + PE) is constant.
Power = work / time = F · v.
The work-energy theorem: net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy.

Momentum and Collisions
Momentum p = mv. Impulse J = FΔt = Δp.
Conservation of momentum: total momentum before a collision equals total momentum after, in an isolated system.
Elastic collisions: both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved.
Inelastic collisions: momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is not (some converts to heat/sound/deformation).
Perfectly inelastic collision: objects stick together after colliding.

Circular Motion and Gravitation
Centripetal acceleration: a_c = v²/r, directed toward the center of the circle.
Centripetal force: F_c = mv²/r — provided by tension, gravity, friction, or normal force.
Newton's law of universal gravitation: F = G(m₁m₂)/r².
Orbital motion: gravity provides the centripetal force for satellites and planets.

Rotational Motion
Angular displacement, velocity, and acceleration are the rotational analogs of linear displacement, velocity, and acceleration.
Torque: τ = rF sin(θ) — the rotational analog of force.
Moment of inertia (I) depends on mass distribution relative to the axis of rotation.
Angular momentum: L = Iω. Conserved when no external torque acts on a system.

Simple Harmonic Motion and Waves
A mass on a spring oscillates with restoring force F = -kx (Hooke's Law).
Period of a spring-mass system: T = 2π√(m/k).
Period of a simple pendulum: T = 2π√(L/g) for small angles.
Wave speed: v = fλ, where f is frequency and λ is wavelength.
Mechanical waves require a medium; transverse waves vibrate perpendicular to travel direction, longitudinal waves vibrate parallel.
