General Chemistry — Unit Review Notes

Atomic Structure
An atom consists of protons (+), neutrons (neutral), and electrons (-).
Atomic number (Z) = number of protons. Mass number (A) = protons + neutrons.
Isotopes: same element, different number of neutrons.
Electrons occupy energy levels (shells); each shell holds up to 2n² electrons.
Valence electrons are in the outermost shell and determine chemical behavior.

The Periodic Table
Elements are arranged by increasing atomic number.
Groups (columns) share similar valence electron configurations and chemical properties.
Periods (rows) represent the highest energy level occupied by electrons.
Trends across a period (left to right): atomic radius decreases, electronegativity increases, ionization energy increases.
Trends down a group: atomic radius increases, electronegativity decreases, ionization energy decreases.

Chemical Bonding
Ionic bonds: transfer of electrons between a metal and a nonmetal, forming charged ions held together by electrostatic attraction.
Covalent bonds: sharing of electron pairs between nonmetals. Can be single, double, or triple bonds.
Polar covalent bonds: unequal sharing of electrons due to differences in electronegativity.
Metallic bonds: a "sea of electrons" shared among metal cations, giving conductivity and malleability.

Chemical Reactions and Equations
A balanced equation has equal numbers of each atom on both sides (conservation of mass).
Types of reactions: synthesis (A + B → AB), decomposition (AB → A + B), single replacement, double replacement, combustion.
Stoichiometry: use mole ratios from the balanced equation to convert between reactants and products.
Limiting reactant: the reactant that runs out first and determines the maximum amount of product.

The Mole and Molar Mass
1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number).
Molar mass (g/mol) = sum of atomic masses in the formula.
moles = mass (g) / molar mass (g/mol)

Acids, Bases, and pH
Acids donate H⁺ ions (protons); bases accept H⁺ ions or donate OH⁻.
pH = -log[H⁺]. pH < 7 is acidic, pH = 7 is neutral, pH > 7 is basic.
Strong acids/bases fully dissociate in water; weak acids/bases only partially dissociate.
Neutralization: acid + base → salt + water.

Gas Laws
Boyle's Law: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ (constant temperature).
Charles's Law: V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ (constant pressure).
Combined gas law: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂.
Ideal gas law: PV = nRT, where R = 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K).

Thermochemistry
Exothermic reactions release energy (ΔH < 0); endothermic reactions absorb energy (ΔH > 0).
Enthalpy (H) is the heat content of a system at constant pressure.
Hess's Law: the total enthalpy change is the same regardless of the number of steps taken.

Reaction Rates and Equilibrium
Reaction rate depends on concentration, temperature, surface area, and presence of a catalyst.
Catalysts speed up reactions by lowering the activation energy without being consumed.
At equilibrium, the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal; concentrations remain constant.
Le Chatelier's Principle: if a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts to counteract the disturbance.
